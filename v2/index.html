    How it works

    MazimaCheck helps you check photos and videos before you share or believe them. Here is how it reaches a verdict and how to read one.

    Reading the score

    AI scoreVerdictOn our 941 test images
    below 45%Likely real79% of images here were real
    45% to under 65%Uncertain62% AI and 38% real, close to a coin toss. About 35% of images land here.
    65% and aboveLikely AI-generated81% of images here were AI

    The model's own decision line is 55%. The uncertain range sits 10 points either side of it. That width is a judgement call: a wider range is more accurate but leaves almost half of all photos without an answer.

    What happens to a photo

    1. Five 224-pixel samples are cut from the photo at full sharpness: the centre and the four corners. The photo is never shrunk first, because shrinking erases the fine traces generators leave.
    2. A ResNet-50 network, pretrained on ImageNet and fine-tuned by us on real and AI images, scores each sample.
    3. The five scores are averaged into the AI score.
    4. The suspicion map runs the same network on a grid of samples across the whole photo.

    How faces are checked

    A compact face finder locates every face and boxes it on the photo. Each face is cut out as a square and scored by a second network trained on OpenForensics, a public set of real and face-swapped photos. It recognises that style of face swap. Faces made with other tools can pass it, so a clear face result is not proof on its own.

    Good to know

    • MazimaCheck is a screening aid, not proof. Check anything important with a second source, such as a reverse image search.
    • Heavily shrunk or re-compressed photos are harder to judge. The Lab shows how much a photo's score moves.
    • Regular textures such as brick walls can raise the score.
    • Some AI images score as real, which is why the uncertain zone and the suspicion map are there.
