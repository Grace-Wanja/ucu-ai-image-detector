What MazimaCheck does

Reads the fine detail

Five full-sharpness samples are cut from your photo and scored. It is never shrunk first, because shrinking erases the traces AI generators leave.

Shows where it looks suspicious

A suspicion map scores a grid of samples across the whole photo, so you can see which areas drive the verdict.

Checks every face

Faces are found and boxed on the photo, and each one gets its own check for face-swap patterns.

Tests it against chat apps

Shrink and compress your photo the way messaging apps do, and watch whether the verdict holds.

Honest about where it stands

What you get today

  • A verdict in three zones, each tied to what we measured on test images
  • A suspicion map showing where the score is highest
  • Face boxes and a check for each face
  • A chat-app stress test and a batch test for evaluators
  • Private by design: nothing leaves your device, and it works offline after the first load

  • More kinds of fake face. A retrained face check that has seen more fake types caught 43 to 65% of types it had never met. It is the next release.
  • Video. The first version is out and has not been evaluated yet.
  • More Ugandan photos in testing.

    How far to trust it

    MazimaCheck helps you check photos and videos before you share or believe them. It is in active development. This page says plainly how well it works today and what we are fixing.

    Reading the score

    AI scoreVerdictOn our 941 test images
    below 45%Likely real79% of images here were real
    45% to under 65%Uncertain62% AI and 38% real, close to a coin toss. About 35% of images land here.
    65% and aboveLikely AI-generated81% of images here were AI

    The model's own decision line is 55%. The uncertain range sits 10 points either side of it. That width is a judgement call: a wider range is more accurate but leaves almost half of all photos without an answer.

    What happens to a photo

    1. Five 224-pixel samples are cut from the photo at full sharpness: the centre and the four corners. The photo is never shrunk first, because shrinking erases the fine traces generators leave.
    2. A ResNet-50 network, pretrained on ImageNet and fine-tuned by us on real and AI images, scores each sample.
    3. The five scores are averaged into the AI score.
    4. The suspicion map runs the same network on a grid of samples across the whole photo.

    Face checks: what we measured

    MazimaCheck finds faces with a small open face finder, cuts each one out and scores it with a second network trained on OpenForensics, a public set of face-swap photos. On that set it scores about 99%, and we checked that none of its test photos had a near-copy in training. Then we tried faces made in other ways, using 300 images of each from the public DeepFakeFace set:

    GroupCalled forgedSeparation from real (AUC)
    Real celebrity photos8% (false alarms)not applicable
    Face swap (InsightFace)5%0.46
    Stable Diffusion inpainting6%0.47
    Stable Diffusion text-to-image9%0.52

    An AUC of 0.5 means no better than guessing. So the current face check recognises the swaps it was trained on and little else, and we show its result as a pattern match, never as proof that a face is real. We then retrained it on OpenForensics plus two of the three DeepFakeFace types and tested it on the third, held out each time. It caught 43% of unseen face swaps, 65% of unseen inpainting and 45% of unseen text-to-image faces, with 6 to 10% false alarms and AUC between 0.78 and 0.88. That retrained check is the next release.

    Known limits, and what we are doing about them

    • It misses AI images often: 38% of AI test images were called real, and 47% of those shared on Reddit.
    • Image size and compression move the score. On our own photos taken in Uganda, shrinking a real photo changed its score by up to 19 points.
    • Regular textures such as brick walls can be flagged as AI.
    • The face check in this version only recognises the face swaps it was trained on. The retrained version described above is next.
    • Our test photos are mostly public and online sources. We are adding more photos taken and shared in Uganda to see how the results change.
    • The video check is a first version and has not been evaluated yet.
    • It is a screening aid, not proof. Check anything important with a second source.
