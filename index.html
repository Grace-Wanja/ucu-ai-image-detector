Is that photo real?

AI can now make a convincing photo in seconds. Drop in a picture and MazimaCheck tells you whether it looks AI-generated, and how sure it is.

Your photo never leaves your phone or computer. The check runs inside your browser.

Getting MazimaCheck ready. The first visit downloads the model (about 94 MB), so use Wi-Fi if you can.

    How it works

    1. Choose a photo. JPEG, PNG or WebP, up to 25 MB.
    2. MazimaCheck looks at it. A trained model scans five patches of the picture for the faint patterns AI tools leave behind.
    3. You get a verdict. The AI score decides it: under is likely real, up to is uncertain, and above is likely AI-generated.

    Reading the score

    Every photo gets an AI score from 0% to 100%: the model's estimated chance that the photo is AI-generated. The verdict follows fixed cut-offs, so the same score always gives the same verdict.

    How we chose the cut-offs: the model's own best-guess line is 55% (picked on the validation photos during training). We placed the uncertain zone 10 points either side of it (45% and 65%). That width is a judgement call, a round number that gave about 80% correct answers outside the zone on the validation photos; the table above then checks it on the test photos. A wider zone would be more accurate but would leave nearly half of all photos uncertain. On photos taken from Reddit the same cut-offs were right 77% and 76% of the time, so expect somewhat less on social-media pictures.

    Batch test

    For evaluators: check a whole set of photos automatically. Choose a folder (or many files), press Start, and MazimaCheck scores every image and draws the confusion matrix. Your photos still stay on your device.

    Labels are read from a labels.csv (columns filename,label, optional role,generator,p_fake) or from folders named real and ai. To use the same test set as our training notebook, run the export cell in the notebook and choose the folder it makes.

    How far to trust it

    MazimaCheck is a screening aid, not proof. It can be wrong, including when it sounds sure, so check anything important with a second source.

    • Photos shared through WhatsApp or screenshots are squeezed and shrunk, which makes the check less reliable.
    • Very small or heavily edited pictures are harder to judge.
    • It looks at still images only. It does not check video or face swaps.
    • It does not point to the part of the picture that looks fake, because our tests showed such heat maps were not reliable.

    MazimaCheck is a student research prototype from Uganda Christian University. It asks a simple question: how well do AI image detectors work on Ugandan photos?
